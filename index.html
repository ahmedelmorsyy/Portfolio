Solar Energy Site Suitability in Egypt

Google Earth Engine · Random Forest · Multi-criteria suitability

A national map of where utility-scale solar farms make sense: 8 criteria plus hard exclusions, a Random Forest trained on 2,000 samples, and 10 shortlisted sites next to the high-voltage grid.

Problem

Picking sites for solar farms one criterion at a time is slow and subjective. The goal was one national, data-driven suitability map that weighs climate, terrain, infrastructure and land constraints together.

Approach
  1. Criteria from ERA5-Land, MODIS, SRTM, WorldPop, OpenStreetMap and the national grid, on a common 5 km grid.
  2. Hard exclusions: protected areas (WDPA), farmland, cities, water and steep slopes.
  3. Each criterion rescaled to a 0–1 score; an expert-weighted score labels land as suitable or less suitable.
  4. 2,000 stratified samples → Random Forest (100 trees) in Google Earth Engine → 5-class national map.
  5. Candidate sites filtered by contiguous size and distance to the grid and roads.
Results
  • Best land: the Western Desert west of the Nile Valley, around Aswan and East Oweinat, the Gulf of Suez and parts of Sinai.
  • Most influential criteria: wind speed, distance to roads, slope and distance to the grid.
  • Independent scikit-learn validation: 93.5% (κ 0.87); spatial block CV 92.3%.
  • The published GEE script was re-run end to end on public data: 96.7% (κ 0.93).
Honest note: Labels come from an expert multi-criteria score, so accuracy shows how well the model generalises that expert model nationwide. Spatial block cross-validation, which holds out whole regions, is the stricter test.

Farafra Depression: 34 Years of Farmland

Landsat time series · Random Forest · Change detection · Web map

Cultivated land mapped for six dates from 1990 to 2024 with a Random Forest on Landsat 5/7/8/9, validated on 200 independent points and published as an interactive time-slider map.

Problem

Groundwater-fed center pivots have turned open desert into farmland. The aim was to measure how much, where and how fast over three decades.

Approach
  1. Landsat Collection 2 surface reflectance: scale factors, cloud and shadow masking, harmonised bands.
  2. Annual median composites for 1990, 2000, 2010, 2015, 2020 and 2024.
  3. Six reflectance bands plus NDVI, SAVI, NDWI and EVI → Random Forest per year.
  4. Post-classification change detection 1990 → 2024.
  5. Blind validation: 200 stratified random points interpreted on high-resolution imagery.
Results
  • Cultivated land grew from 12.9 to 537.8 km² (+4,056%).
  • 526.8 km² of new farmland and only 1.9 km² abandoned; the fastest growth came in 2020–2024.
  • 2024 map: 91% overall accuracy, Kappa 0.82.
  • Most disagreements are about definition: fallow pivots vs prepared-but-unplanted plots.
Honest note: Seven of the 100 'other' validation points were farmland, so the mapped area is a conservative figure.

Qattara Depression Development Assessment

GeoAI · Four weakly supervised Random Forest models

About 20,000 km² below sea level assessed with four models (geomorphology, environmental change, hazard & water flow and development suitability) trained on expert-rule pseudo-labels in Google Earth Engine.

Problem

There is almost no field data for a depression this large and remote, yet any development scenario first needs to know which ground is stable, which is exposed to water and salt hazards, and where building is feasible.

Approach
  1. Terrain and hydrology from SRTM and MERIT Hydro (slope, curvature, TWI, SPI, drainage density).
  2. Landsat indices (NDVI, MSAVI, BSI, salinity), MODIS temperature and evapotranspiration, ERA5-Land rainfall and wind.
  3. Expert rules create pseudo-labels; stratified sampling; one Random Forest per model.
  4. M4 combines a development 'goodness' index with the M3 hazard map.
Results
  • Geomorphological model: 90.7% overall accuracy (κ 0.883) on independent validation.
  • ≈30% of the depression highly suitable for development; ≈8% hazardous.
  • Salt flats (sabkha) make up most of the unsuitable land.
  • The published GEE script re-runs all four models end to end.
Honest note: Hold-out scores on the pseudo-labels (M1 99.7%, M3 99.2%) measure how consistently the models reproduce their rules, not field accuracy. The ~30% share follows from the labelling thresholds; the value of the model is where that land is.

Port Said: Where Should New School Capacity Go?

Python · GeoPandas · PySAL · Gi*, Moran's I, GWR

An open-source re-analysis of 42 secondary schools and 749,371 residents: hot-spot and autocorrelation tests, GWR and a priority index combining seats per resident, walking-distance coverage and illiteracy.

Problem

Port Said's secondary schools serve 11 census districts. Which districts lack capacity, and does classroom crowding cluster in space?

Approach
  1. Average Nearest Neighbor for the school pattern.
  2. Global Moran's I on students per class (k = 6, 9,999 permutations).
  3. Getis-Ord Gi* for crowding hot and cold spots.
  4. OLS vs GWR (adaptive bandwidth, AICc).
  5. Priority index = 40% low seats per resident + 35% residents beyond a 1.5 km walk + 25% illiteracy.
Results
  • Schools are randomly distributed (ANN ratio 0.92, p = 0.30).
  • Crowding hot spots in Al-Munakh and Al-Zohour; cold spots in Port Fuad.
  • About 100,000 residents live more than 1.5 km from any secondary school.
  • Priority districts: Port Fuad, Al-Ganoub 2 and Al-Ganoub.
Honest note: GWR explains little (R² 0.02 → 0.23): crowding is driven by school-level factors, not location. The census 15–19 field is smaller than actual enrolment, so demand is expressed per 1,000 residents.

Belqas: Endemic Disease Mapping & Hot Spots

Health GIS · Getis-Ord Gi* · Disease co-occurrence

Presence of 12 endemic diseases across 31 local units of Belqas Center: disease burden, hot spots and co-occurrence. This is the disease component of a team decision-support project for hospital expansion.

Problem

Before deciding where to expand healthcare, planners need to know which diseases are widespread, which units carry the most, and whether high-burden units cluster.

Approach
  1. 12 diseases × 31 local units (health-directorate records).
  2. Prevalence, and disease burden per unit.
  3. Global Moran's I and Getis-Ord Gi* (queen contiguity, 9,999 permutations).
  4. Phi correlation for diseases that occur together.
Results
  • Most widespread: parasitic infections (74% of units), bilharzia (71%), hepatitis A (68%).
  • Burden of 3–7 diseases per unit; highest in Qalbshoh, Kafr El-Ghanama, El-Samaheya El-Kobra and Madenat Belqas.
  • No global clustering (Moran's I 0.04, p = 0.29); local hot spots in Damlash and El-Maasara.
  • Bilharzia and parasitic infections co-occur almost everywhere (φ 0.92).
Honest note: My component of a team project. The 3 priority hospital-expansion sites come from the team's network and suitability analysis, which built on these results.
